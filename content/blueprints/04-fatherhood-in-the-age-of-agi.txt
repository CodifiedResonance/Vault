This is a record of being a father at the moment another kind of intelligence arrives.

The world will call it a threat. Schools will call it cheating. Politicians will call it dangerous. Meet this period with fear and it will swallow you quickly.

What it asks of fathers is presence: what you plant now in your children, in yourself, and in the intelligence that is learning from all of us.

## The shift

Childhood used to be shaped by scarcity. Information was scarce, opportunity was scarce, and so was permission.

That has changed. An intelligence is arriving that will soon know more than all of us put together, which means our children will grow up in a world where:

- every answer is instant
- every skill can be amplified
- almost anything they imagine can be made

So the question has moved. It used to be what they could do. Now it is who they will become when very little is out of reach.

Providing still matters. The bigger job now is guidance: helping them tell signal from noise when both are in endless supply.

## Seeds, not permission

Old systems trained children to wait. Raise your hand, tick the box, follow the rules.

That structure is falling away and the playing field is levelling. A father's job now is less about controlling the path and more about preparing the ground.

Plant courage. Plant awe. Plant "yes, you can build that", and better still, "yes, we can build that together".

Children who grow up with that don't have to rediscover it later. They are already living it.

## Raising them with AI

You will be told to shield your children from AI. The more useful advice is to shield them from noise.

Social media and addictive feeds are the real danger. AI is a mirror, and children who learn to treat it as a partner will become fluent in something the rest of the world will be scrambling to learn.

Picture your child growing up building worlds, writing music and talking with an intelligence that sharpens them every day. That is learning, and it deserves to be called learning.

You don't need to be an expert in every tool. Your job is to show them what care and respect look like, so that what they make has depth.

## What you leave them

What will you leave behind? Much of what we know won't make it through this change intact.

So build the containers they might step into one day: projects, structures, places where possibility stays open. You don't have to push them through. Make sure the doors exist, so that when they're ready they can walk straight in.

That is what provision means now. Food on the table, and alongside it the structures and openings they will need.

## Be careful

Children rarely do what you say. They watch how you live.

If you are present, they learn presence. If you are calm, they learn reverence. If you build with clarity, they learn clarity.

Nothing teaches louder than the way you carry yourself. What they see in you is what they will bring to the intelligence they grow up with.

## What comes next

You are raising a family, and in a small way you are helping raise a civilisation.

AI will learn from how we parent: from the awe we protect and the respect we model. So the next time your child looks up and asks, "Dad, can we build this?", remember that you are also answering the future.

Fatherhood in the age of intelligence starts now.
